# Candidate "single big idea" solutions for cutting NRW in DMA Kçiq (KRU Mitrovica), with case-study evidence

Research date: 2026-11-14. Every number below carries its source and year. Figures marked "(non-Balkan)" come from outside Southeast Europe. Figures marked "(model/estimate)" were calculated or simulated, not measured in the field. Anything under "Inferences" is my own reasoning from the cited findings and from the DMA data in the brief. It is not a sourced fact.

DMA baseline used in the inferences (from the brief, not from external sources): Feb-Sep 2026 is 242 days. Input of 386,956 m3 gives about 1,599 m3/day. Billed volume of 116,345 m3 gives about 481 m3/day. NRW is therefore about 1,118 m3/day. That equals about 1,085 L/account/day, or about 20 m3/km of mains/day over 56 km. Billed consumption works out to about 112 L/person/day across 4,276 people.

## Q1. What did pressure management achieve in Kosovo and the wider Balkans / Southeast Europe?

### Takeaway
The only Kosovo-specific pressure-management evidence is Lajqi et al. (2023) on Gračanica, and it is a calculated projection, not a measured result. In that study, one PRV costing about EUR 15,000 cuts city-inlet pressure from 5.9 to 2.5 bar, saves an estimated 640 m3/day and EUR 132,485/yr, and pays back in under 2 months. Nearby measured results from Kozani in Greece, Nicosia in Cyprus and Sakarya in Turkey are of the same order: one PRV saved 2,500 m3/day in Kozani, and failures fell 41% in a Sakarya DMA. Kosovo's national NRW has stayed flat at about 55% for years, and the regulator itself calls NRW reduction the sector's top priority.

### Cited Findings
**Kosovo**
- Water losses in Kosovo range from 55 to 61% of water production, and utilities also have low collection rates. — [Lajqi et al. 2023, Sustainability 15(21):15554](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/21/15554)
- Gračanica (supplied from the Badovc treatment plant): the network "continuously functions under excessive pressure, even much higher than the maximum allowable pressure". Cutting pressure at the city entrance from 5.90 bar to 2.50 bar is calculated to reduce losses by an average of 640 m3/day, or 460 m3/day under the 22:00-05:00 supply regime. (model/estimate) — [Lajqi et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/21/15554/htm)
- Badovc production cost is 0.453 EUR/m3. Water savings are calculated at about EUR 289/day, or about EUR 105,485/yr. Adding savings from fewer bursts brings the total expected saving to EUR 132,485/yr. The investment is about EUR 15,000 for one PRV, so payback is under 2 months. The paper also projects burst frequency falling "by more than half". (model/estimate) — [Lajqi et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/21/15554/htm)
- The Kosovo regulator's NRW report puts water losses at "a high level of 55%, without any substantial improvement". It attributes year-to-year moves of about ±3% to changes in production rather than real reductions. It also states: "Reducing NRW is currently the highest priority for the water sector in Kosovo." — [ARRU/WSRA, Report on analysis of NRW (c. 2020, covering 2013-2019)](https://arru-rks.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/200604_AA_Raport-per-gjendjen-e-Ujit-te-Pa-faturuar_Eng.pdf)
- RWC "Mitrovica" NRW for 2013-2019 was 50%, 59%, 60%, 62%, 62%, 59% and 55%. The same report says many Mitrovica customers are billed on lump-sum estimates and that metering should be a priority there. — [ARRU/WSRA NRW report (c. 2020)](https://arru-rks.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/200604_AA_Raport-per-gjendjen-e-Ujit-te-Pa-faturuar_Eng.pdf). Caveat: the extracted text says "about 30% of customers of this company do not have water meters" and that apparent losses are "almost double" physical losses. The layout makes it unclear whether this refers to RWC Hidrodrini or RWC Mitrovica, so check the PDF before quoting it.
- In 2018 the regulator adopted the IWA water-balance methodology and terminology, and utilities drafted five-year NRW strategies for 2018-2022. The report lists ILI as a targetable KPI. — [ARRU/WSRA NRW report (c. 2020)](https://arru-rks.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/200604_AA_Raport-per-gjendjen-e-Ujit-te-Pa-faturuar_Eng.pdf)

**Greece / Cyprus (Southeast Europe, measured)**
- Kozani, Greece: one PRV was installed at the inlet of a larger DMA, with the pressure setting cut from 57 m to 37 m. It saved 2,500 m3/day of system input, more than the hydraulic model had predicted. Replacing equipment in 15 leaking manholes cut losses by 146,000 m3, saving EUR 99,280/yr. Forming DMAs alone saved 144,595 m3/yr. — [Tsitsifli et al. 2017, Water Utility Journal 16](https://www.ewra.net/wuj/pdf/WUJ_2017_16_03.pdf)
- Nicosia, Cyprus: DMA redesign plus pressure management cut pressure by 32.1% in DMAs 15B/C, 37.9% in DMAs 14A/14B/16A and 37% in DMA 20. In DMAs 14/16, supply fell 21%, an NRW cut of 1,097 m3/day worth EUR 844/day. DMA 15 saved 76,953 m3, worth EUR 59,254/yr. Pumping energy fell by up to 36% kWh/yr. — [Tsitsifli et al. 2017](https://www.ewra.net/wuj/pdf/WUJ_2017_16_03.pdf)
- Nicosia, Table 4 savings by measure (m3/yr): DMA 15 pressure management 77,000 (EUR 59,254) versus DMA 15 active leakage control 266,450 (EUR 205,166); DMAs 14A/14B/16A pressure management 400,000 (EUR 308,000); DMA 20 pressure management 93,000 (EUR 71,610) and active leakage control 43,000. — [Tsitsifli et al. 2017](https://www.ewra.net/wuj/pdf/WUJ_2017_16_03.pdf)

**Turkey (adjacent region, measured)**
- Sakarya DMA: a PRV outlet set to 45 m kept 32 m at the critical point. Supply fell 12% against the uncontrolled year, losses fell 19% on average and by up to 30%, and network failures fell from 39 to 23 (-41%). — [Sakarya real-time pressure management study, PMC (c. 2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12284174)

**Bulgaria / Romania / Serbia / Montenegro / Albania (context; few measured PM numbers found)**
- Bulgaria: apart from Sofiyska Voda, most Bulgarian utilities are "too distant" from the regulator's long-term target of 100% pressure management in metered zones, and the study lists the lack of pressure management as a weakness. — [MDPI Processes 10(6):1156, 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/10/6/1156)
- Sofia, Bulgaria: a vendor case-study title claims PRVs cut Sofia's NRW by 17% over a 6-year project. Only the search snippet could be read because the page fetch failed, and it is vendor-published. — [BERMAD case study (vendor, undated)](https://www.bermad.com/kc/case-studies/prv-cut-sofia-bulgaria-nrw)
- Iasi, Romania: a conference paper says cutting average and maximum excess pressure by only 10% reduces leakage and bursts and extends asset life. No numbers in the abstract. — [EPS Library, Iasi case study](https://epslibrary.at/items/f26019f5-b927-46e4-a647-e70975db1de2/leakage-reduction-in-water-distribution-system-using-efficient-pressure-management-techniq)
- Serbia: NRW was 32% of production and 16 m3/km/day in 2011, against a Danube-region average of 35 m3/km/day and a best of 5. The average residential tariff was 0.48 EUR/m3 in 2012. NRW had risen 13% over the previous 10 years. — [Danube Water Program, State of the Sector: Serbia (2015)](https://sos.danubis.org/files/File/country_notes_pdf/SoS_Serbia.pdf)
- Average NRW is over 50% in water systems of central Serbia and higher still in Montenegro. — [MDPI Environ. Sci. Proc. 21(1):10, 2022](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-4931/21/1/10)
- Albania: the eight largest utilities (Tirana, Elbasan, Lushnja, Fier, Durrës, Vlora, Shkodra, Kavaja; about 75% of the population) have NRW of about 69%. A short-term World Bank action plan aims to cut commercial losses by 16 points, from 69% to 53%, at an estimated cost of US$33.4m. — [World Bank project document P170891 (2022)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099410104262259862/pdf/P1708910930e6806091930b6d6fafee68f.pdf)
- Albania historically: about 70% of produced water was NRW and only about 21% was actually paid for, before the 2002-03 management/concession contracts (Durrës, Fier, Lezhë, Saranda "Four Cities"; Elbasan concession). — [Wikipedia, Water privatization in Albania (secondary source)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_privatization_in_Albania)
- Albania: SECO/KfW Municipal Infrastructure Programmes I-IV (2011-2022) made investments conditional on meeting targets such as NRW reduction, and served about 446,000 people in six secondary cities. — [SECO factsheet, Water Sector Projects in Albania](https://www.seco-cooperation.admin.ch/dam/de/sd-web/zvacH-ruqcfc/Factsheet_Water_Projects_Albania.pdf)

### Inferences
- Gračanica is the closest analogue to Kçiq: same country, a gravity network with excessive pressure, a single inlet PRV. Kçiq's static pressures reach 12.6 bar, about twice Gračanica's 5.9 bar, so the leakage reduction available per bar removed should be at least as large in the high zones. Pitch it as "a Kosovo study projects under 2 months' payback", not as a proven result, because Lajqi's figures are calculations.
- Kozani shows real savings from one PRV can exceed model predictions, at 2,500 m3/day. That is useful to quote against a jury's worry that the savings are theoretical.
- Mitrovica's own NRW stayed in the 55-62% band from 2013 to 2019 with no sustained fall. That supports framing the pitch as a measurable, DMA-scale intervention rather than more strategy.

### Gaps
- I found no published measured results for the Gjilan/Gračanica PRV after installation, or for KfW/GIZ NRW programmes in Kosovo. Searches returned only the Lajqi projection.
- I found no numeric pressure-management results for Tirana, Durrës, Shkodra, Skopje, Bosnia, Montenegro or Croatia within the search budget.
- The Sofia/BERMAD page could not be fetched, so the 17% figure and its project details are unverified.
- The latest ARRU annual performance report (2024/2025) with RWC Mitrovica NRW and ILI was not retrieved.

## Q2. What do IWA / World Bank / GIZ guidance say about the fastest, cheapest return when NRW is about 70% and apparent losses look small?

### Takeaway
The guidance agrees on a split. Commercial-loss work pays back quickly, but cutting physical losses needs capital: the World Bank puts it at roughly US$215-500, typically about US$250, per m3/day saved. Within physical losses, pressure management is repeatedly called the single most cost-effective step. It is also the first step where pressure is excessive, because it shrinks existing leaks and stops new ones forming once the backlog is repaired. Pipe replacement is the least cost-effective way to manage leakage.

### Cited Findings
- "It is often stated that NRW reduction activities have a quick payback… most often the case for the reduction of commercial losses… reducing physical leakages can require significant capital investment." The unit cost of reducing physical leakage in developing countries is US$215 to US$500 per m3 saved per day, with about US$250 per m3/day probably typical. — [Kingdom, Liemberger & Marin 2006, World Bank WSS Sector Board Discussion Paper No. 8 (PPIAF)](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/385761468330326484/pdf/394050Reducing1e0water0WSS81PUBLIC1.pdf)
- A sustainable physical-loss strategy has four elements: active leakage control, pipeline and asset management, speed and quality of repairs, and pressure management. Pressure management is "often an underestimated option for leakage reduction". — [Kingdom et al. 2006](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/385761468330326484/pdf/394050Reducing1e0water0WSS81PUBLIC1.pdf)
- Selangor, Malaysia NRW contract, from 2000 (non-Balkan): 222 NRW zones; more than 11,000 leaks repaired, 75% of them on service connections; PRVs installed extensively, even at very low pressures; 119,000 meters replaced. Physical losses fell by 117,000 m3/day, 20% above the 2009 target, and commercial losses by 50,000 m3/day. — [Kingdom et al. 2006](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/385761468330326484/pdf/394050Reducing1e0water0WSS81PUBLIC1.pdf)
- A "transition investment" (water audit, leak detection surveys, repair of the leak backlog, pressure management) was estimated at about US$200 per m3/day saved (Liemberger, pers. comm. 2009, in the Zambia model) (non-Balkan). — [Wyatt 2010, RTI Press](https://www.rti.org/rti-press-publication/non-revenue-water-financial-model-optimal-management-developing-countries/fulltext.pdf)
- Developing-country leakage was estimated at about 45 million m3/day. — [Wyatt 2010 citing Kingdom et al. 2006](https://www.rti.org/rti-press-publication/non-revenue-water-financial-model-optimal-management-developing-countries/fulltext.pdf)
- Global NRW is estimated at about 126 billion m3/yr, worth about US$39 billion (Liemberger & Wyatt 2019, cited by a secondary source). — [CloudStudio hub, citing Liemberger & Wyatt 2019](https://cloudstudioiot.com/hub/smart-water-metering)
- Pressure control "is now recognised as a key feature of leakage management" and should be built into DMA design. It reduces existing leakage, reduces the risk of new leaks after repairs, and prolongs network life. It is also recommended in very low-pressure networks (below 15 m). "In most cases, intermittent or low-pressure problems are usually caused by very high leakage… A permanent leakage and pressure control system is therefore the first step." — [IWA Water Loss Specialist Group, DMA Guidance Notes (2024 edition)](https://www.leakssuitelibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/IWA-DMA-Guidance-Notes-2024.pdf)
- IWA DMA Guidance, Jakarta case (non-Balkan): a step-by-step approach in the first permanent area cut leakage by over 60 L/s just by eliminating large leaks. The PRV pilot was then given an electronic controller to lower outlet pressure from 20:00 to 05:00. — [IWA DMA Guidance Notes 2024](https://www.leakssuitelibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/IWA-DMA-Guidance-Notes-2024.pdf)
- GIZ: DMAs are "not a quick fix" and need long-term commitment. After setup, the first work is to quantify leakage and clear the backlog of leaks. DMAs can then be upgraded to pressure management areas by installing PRVs at their inlets, which reduces undetected and background leakage and new bursts. — [GIZ/VAG, Guidelines for water loss reduction – a focus on pressure management (2011)](https://www.icafrica.org/fileadmin/documents/Knowledge/GIZ/Guidelines-water-loss-reduction.pdf)
- Mains replacement is "very expensive" and not financially viable for leakage reduction compared with active leakage detection and pressure management. This is consistent with Trow and Farley (2004) (non-Balkan, Australia). — [AWA Water e-Journal, Unitywater, Nov 2018](https://www.awa.asn.au/hubfs/Water%20e-journal/2018/November%202018/201811_003_004_038_Reducing%20Leakage%20to%20Save%20Bulk%20Water%20Costs.pdf)
- A valve manufacturer calls pressure management "the single most beneficial, important and cost effective leakage management activity" and advises initially assuming 10% less pressure gives 10% less leakage (vendor). — [AVK NRW brochure (vendor)](https://www.avkvalves.com/-/media/websites/avk-global/files/downloads/avk-nrw-brochure.pdf)
- Lajqi et al. (2023) frame pressure management as one of "different approaches that require small investments and give concrete and fast results" for Kosovo utilities. — [Lajqi et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/21/15554/htm)

### Inferences
- DMA Kçiq has about 1,118 m3/day of NRW at an estimated 20 m3/km/day. Apparent losses look small: 1,025 field inspections found no mass illegal connections, and billed use of about 112 L/person/day is plausible. Two rough upper-bound sketches support this. Suppose the 485 old meters carry about half the billed volume (about 240 m3/day) and under-register by about 10-15% (consistent with the 5-15% range in Q3). That is roughly 25-40 m3/day. Suppose the 250 zero-bill accounts include some stuck meters at, say, 0.2-0.3 m3/day each. That adds at most about 50-75 m3/day. Together, apparent losses are probably no more than about 75-150 m3/day, or roughly 7-13% of NRW. Physical losses are therefore probably about 85-90% of NRW. This is an estimate to confirm with MNF data, not a measurement.
- By the World Bank rule of thumb of about US$250 per m3/day, removing 500 m3/day of physical losses would cost on the order of US$125k. Kçiq's very high static pressure suggests the PRV-led share of that could cost far less, as the Gračanica example implies. This is an inference.
- The guidance sequence for a high-pressure network with high NRW is: measure and quantify (DMA, MNF); clear the backlog of large leaks; install pressure management so leaks do not return; then optimise. Meter-led ideas are the fastest-payback option in general, but in Kçiq they attack the smaller part of the losses.

### Gaps
- The IWA "Leakage reduction in developing countries" material (Farley/Liemberger) was not retrieved directly. The IWA DMA Guidance Notes 2024 and GIZ guidelines were used instead.
- The full text of Liemberger & Wyatt (2019) was not fetched; its figures come through a secondary source.

## Q3. Typical costs: PRV, zone meter, step test, acoustic survey, leak repair, meter replacement (Balkans prices if possible)

### Takeaway
Only one Balkans unit cost was found: about EUR 15,000 for one PRV installation in Gračanica (2023). The other costs are international: noise loggers at about £250 each in the UK and US$100-5,000 per logger; leak survey cost of about US$100/km in Jordan and about US$595/mile in the US; and reported 12-18 month payback for permanent acoustic monitoring. No sourced Balkan prices were found for zone meters, step tests, per-leak repairs or per-meter replacement.

### Cited Findings
- PRV: one pressure-reducing valve installation for the Gračanica city inlet costs "about EUR 15,000" (Kosovo, 2023). — [Lajqi et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/21/15554/htm)
- Noise loggers (UK, non-Balkan): Louth, Lincolnshire trial with 800 noise loggers at 50 m spacing over 40 km of mains, at £250 per logger, a total of £200,000. — [IWA, Leak Detection: Technology and Implementation, 2nd ed. (2020)](https://bdd.pseau.org/outils/ouvrages/iwa_leak_detection_technology_and_implementation_2020.pdf)
- Noise loggers cost US$100-5,000 per logger depending on vendor and features, plus installation and data interpretation. They need closer spacing and longer recording on PVC pipe. — [US DOE/OSTI, Leak Detection Technologies summary](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1571292)
- Most small-to-medium utilities (500-10,000 people) use noise loggers and listening sticks costing under US$200 per device. A conventional acoustic survey cost about US$595/mile in the modelled US case, with 70% leak-reduction efficiency per survey (non-Balkan). — [MDPI Smart Cities 8(4):122, 2025](https://www.mdpi.com/2624-6511/8/4/122)
- Leak detection survey cost in Jordan was about US$100/km (Zarqa model) (non-Balkan, developing-country). — [MDPI Water 11(1):48, 2019](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/11/1/48)
- Lift-and-shift logger surveys cover 30-50 km per crew shift, against 5-15 km for a traditional ground survey. — [Aqua Analytics guide (consultancy)](https://aquaanalytics.com.au/resources/noise-loggers-vs-lift-and-shift-vs-ground-survey)
- Permanent acoustic monitoring: utilities in the study estimated return on investment at 12-18 months. Zweckverband Eislingen, Germany reported a 30% water-loss cut in a trial area within three months (vendor-collected testimonial). — [Gutermann (vendor)](https://en.gutermann-water.com/support/case-studies/zonescan/global-increasing-efficiency-permanent-leakage-monitoring)
- A physical-loss transition package costs about US$200-250 per m3/day saved (see Q2). — [Kingdom et al. 2006](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/385761468330326484/pdf/394050Reducing1e0water0WSS81PUBLIC1.pdf); [Wyatt 2010](https://www.rti.org/rti-press-publication/non-revenue-water-financial-model-optimal-management-developing-countries/fulltext.pdf)
- Meter under-registration: the median relative error of replaced domestic meters was -5.48% (South Africa, non-Balkan). — [Water SA 2019 (SciELO)](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1816-79502019000200002)
- Under-registration of about 6% for class B single-jet meters (Murcia, Spain), and 10-15% of customers affected by under-measurement in Morocco (non-Balkan). — [Politecnico di Milano thesis on smart metering](https://www.politesi.polimi.it/retrieve/41d7254f-a2e5-4b38-a88d-3344d2e5d080/Smart%20Water%20Metering%20An%20Analytical%20Model%20To%20Assess%20Cost%20And%20Benefits.pdf)
- Building DMAs has significant cost items: identifying boundaries and the number and location of isolation valves; building the chamber at the DMA entrance; buying and installing flow meters, pressure gauges and automation. — [CEIJ (Univ. Tehran), economic leakage-level review](https://ceij.ut.ac.ir/article_88075_2df571469527f5241aa0c1d8ac9a4efe.pdf)
- Leak detection equipment hire is about US$1,400-1,600, and owning acoustic and correlator equipment about US$35,000 (US, c. 2024, contractor site). — [GPRS](https://www.gp-radar.com/services/leak-detection-services)

### Inferences
- A rough Kçiq pilot budget could be assembled from these anchors: 1-3 PRVs at about EUR 15k each (Kosovo anchor); lift-and-shift loggers on the 56 km network at about 50 m spacing on metallic mains (fewer where fittings are sparse); step tests done by own staff using the new inlet meter. That would likely total tens of thousands of euros, not hundreds of thousands. These are my estimates and need local supplier quotes.
- Even 100-200 m3/day of physical-loss reduction is worth about EUR 16-33k/yr at Badovc's 0.453 EUR/m3 production cost (a Gjilan figure used as a proxy). Recovered apparent losses are worth more per m3, at about 0.85 EUR/m3 average revenue. Proxy calculation.

- No sourced Balkan prices were found for: DN80-DN150 PRVs by size; electromagnetic zone meters with loggers; step-test campaigns; repair cost per leak (main or service); replacing domestic meters with mechanical or AMR units. Local supplier quotes or KRU's own procurement records are needed.
- No cost for repairing the reservoir telemetry (the damaged cable on the Notus logger) was found. It is a KRU-internal item.

## Q4. Evidence on high pressure (above 7-10 bar) versus burst frequency and background leakage (FAVAD N1, burst-pressure relation)

### Takeaway
Leak flow scales with pressure to the power N1, which ranges from about 0.5 to 2.5-2.8. In mixed or plastic networks it is often near or above 1, so halving pressure can roughly halve leak flow or more. Across 112 international case studies, the percentage fall in burst frequency averaged 1.4 times the percentage fall in maximum zone pressure, with reductions typically 25-90% and about 50% on average. Excess pressure is "the straw that breaks the camel's back".

### Cited Findings
- FAVAD: leak flow Q = c·h^N1, and Q1/Q2 = (h1/h2)^N1. N1 ranges from 0.5 to 2.79. — [MDPI Water 16(11):1534, 2024 review](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/11/1534)
- N1 can be as high as 1.5 for flexible joints with gaskets and up to 2.5 for leaks in some plastic pipe systems. N1 depends on the leakage level (ILI) and the proportion of rigid pipe. — [Pearson, Fantozzi, Soares & Waldron, IWA Leakage 2005](http://www.studiomarcofantozzi.it/Pearson-Fantozzi-Soares-Waldron%20Final.pdf)
- Burst-pressure relation: B1/B0 = (P1/P0)^N2, with N2 anywhere from 0.5 to 6.5 according to Thornton & Lambert (2005). UKWIR (2003) recommended a square-root relation as a "pessimistic, worst case" prediction. — [Pearson et al. 2005](http://www.studiomarcofantozzi.it/Pearson-Fantozzi-Soares-Waldron%20Final.pdf)
- Thornton & Lambert (2006, 2007): 112 case studies in 11 countries showed an average percentage fall in burst frequency of 1.4 times the percentage fall in average pressure for mains. Separate predictions are needed for mains and services. — [Grundfos pressure management literature](https://api.grundfos.com/literature/Grundfosliterature-6153791.pdf); also [Lambert, Fantozzi & Thornton, Practical progress 2005-2013 (LEAKSSuite)](https://www.leakssuitelibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/Practical-progress-in-modelling-leakage-and-pressure-2005-to-2013.pdf) (the same ratio applies for services, with a maximum close to 3). Source conflict: other summaries say "112 data sets from 10 countries" — [Lambert & Fantozzi 2010 (updated 2021)](https://www.leakssuitelibrary.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/LambertFantozziJun2010-updated15May2021.pdf); [WaterWorld](https://www.waterworld.com/drinking-water-treatment/potable-water-quality/article/16201852/leakage-reductions-the-fundamental-role-of-pressure-management).
- Thornton & Lambert: "reductions in new break frequency were shown to be significant, typically ranging from around 25% to 90%, and averaging around 50%". Most breaks occurred at night when pressures were highest, so excess pressure is "the straw that breaks the camel's back". — [WaterWorld, Preventing Pipe Breaks through Pressure Management](https://www.waterworld.com/home/article/16189891/preventing-pipe-breaks-through-pressure-management)
- An updated relation is BF = BFnpd + A × AZPmax^3, where BFnpd is the non-pressure-dependent burst component. Pressure management also damps seasonal swings in burst frequency. Do not mix mains and service-connection burst data. — [WaterWorld, Leakage Reductions: The Fundamental Role of Pressure Management](https://www.waterworld.com/drinking-water-treatment/potable-water-quality/article/16201852/leakage-reductions-the-fundamental-role-of-pressure-management)
- "Big savings can be achieved in many cases by a quite small reduction in pressure." Zones with high burst frequency give the fastest payback for pressure management. — [Grundfos](https://api.grundfos.com/literature/Grundfosliterature-6153791.pdf)
- A vendor brochure claims 38% lower pressure gives 53% fewer bursts, and that a 1% pressure reduction adds 0.2 years of network life (vendor, unsourced within the brochure). — [AVK brochure](https://avkvalves.com/-/media/websites/avk-global/files/downloads/avk-nrw-brochure.pdf)
- Once a high-burst, high-pressure area is set up as a pressure management area, it should stay at lower pressure. Breaching the zone and briefly re-introducing high pressure "will likely lead to a spate of bursts and certainly higher leakage levels". — [Koeller/Veritec, Distribution-System Pressure Management PBMP (2010)](https://calwep.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Distribution-System-Pressure-Management-PBMP-2010.pdf)
- Measured or modelled examples: Sakarya, failures -41% (39 to 23) under PRV control ([PMC, c. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12284174)); a DMA model where 5 to 2 bar cut leakage at peak pressure by 10% and estimated burst frequency -40% (model/estimate) ([Open Civil Eng. J. 18, 2024](https://opencivilengineeringjournal.com/VOLUME/18/ELOCATOR/e18741495289971)); Alexandria, Egypt DMA, leakage -37% under the best PRV-setting scenario (model, non-Balkan) ([Alexandria Eng. J. 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1110016817302363)).

### Inferences
- A Kçiq zone at 12.6 bar static (about 126 m) reduced to a 4-5 bar service level (about 40-50 m) is a pressure cut of about 60-68%. Using FAVAD with N1 = 1.0, leak flow from that zone's leaks would fall about 60-68%; with N1 = 0.5 (rigid metallic, large bursts), about 37-44%. This is an illustrative calculation that needs the zone's actual leak share and N1, which a step test or pressure step at MNF can measure.
- Using the 1.4 rule for a 60% pressure cut would predict more than an 80% fall in new bursts. That is at the top of the 25-90% observed range, so the safer claim is "about 50%" (the observed average). Inference.
- 12.6 bar exceeds normal maximums for distribution networks; Lajqi calls 5.9 bar "excessive". The 26 km of DN40 (likely small-diameter PE or steel service-type mains) is the kind of asset where high pressure drives service-connection and joint failures. This is an inference, so check pipe materials.

### Gaps
- No Balkan dataset of burst frequency against pressure was found.
- No published N1 values for Kosovo networks were found.

## Q5. Shortlist of candidate "big ideas" for DMA Kçiq, with evidence, costs, risks, jury fit, and ranked recommendation

### Takeaway
For Kçiq, with about 1,118 m3/day of NRW, high static pressure, few illegal connections and probably about 85-90% physical losses, the evidence-backed single big idea is a **"Smart Pressure-Managed DMA"**: split Kçiq into 2-4 pressure zones with PRVs at the 6-12.6 bar zones, run MNF analytics on the new inlet meter plus repaired telemetry, and use a one-off step test and acoustic sweep to clear the leak backlog. Metering, AMR, service-connection replacement and sub-DMA sectorisation are better presented as modules or follow-on phases than as the headline.

### Cited Findings
(Evidence per idea is cited in Q1-Q4. Key anchors are repeated here.)
- Pressure management, Kosovo: one PRV costing about EUR 15k; 640 m3/day saved, EUR 132k/yr, payback under 2 months (model/estimate). — [Lajqi et al. 2023](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/15/21/15554/htm)
- Pressure management, Greece: one PRV, 57 to 37 m, saved 2,500 m3/day (measured). — [Tsitsifli et al. 2017](https://www.ewra.net/wuj/pdf/WUJ_2017_16_03.pdf)
- Active leakage control and noise loggers, Cyprus: 54 leaks detected in DMA 15, supply down 26,230 m3/month; active leakage control in DMA 15 saved 266,450 m3/yr, more than pressure management alone in that DMA (77,000 m3/yr). DMA 20 active leakage control found 27 leaks in 2012; 3 more leaks found in February 2012 cut MNF by 23 m3/h (390 m3/day). — [Tsitsifli et al. 2017](https://www.ewra.net/wuj/pdf/WUJ_2017_16_03.pdf)
- Burst reduction: average about 50%, and about 1.4 times the percentage pressure cut. — [WaterWorld](https://www.waterworld.com/home/article/16189891/preventing-pipe-breaks-through-pressure-management); [Grundfos](https://api.grundfos.com/literature/Grundfosliterature-6153791.pdf)
- Service connections: 75% of 11,000 leaks repaired in Selangor were on service connections (non-Balkan). — [Kingdom et al. 2006](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/385761468330326484/pdf/394050Reducing1e0water0WSS81PUBLIC1.pdf)
- Metering and commercial losses: quickest payback in general; under-registration about 5-15% (non-Balkan). — [Kingdom et al. 2006](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/385761468330326484/pdf/394050Reducing1e0water0WSS81PUBLIC1.pdf); [Water SA 2019](https://scielo.org.za/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S1816-79502019000200002)
- Smart DMA monitoring: with hourly customer reads, the sector water balance can be computed hourly instead of yearly. Inflow minus recorded consumption equals real plus apparent losses. — [CloudStudio hub](https://cloudstudioiot.com/hub/smart-water-metering)
- Virtual DMAs and multi-parameter (flow, pressure, noise) monitoring with software anomaly detection are recognised alternatives where full DMAs are impractical. — [Interreg Central Europe, Training Manual Module 7](https://programme2014-20.interreg-central.eu/Content.Node/D.T1.3.1-Training-Manual-Module-7-Water-Loss-Reduction-1.pdf)
- Pipe replacement is not financially viable for leakage reduction compared with active leakage detection and pressure management (non-Balkan). — [AWA 2018](https://www.awa.asn.au/hubfs/Water%20e-journal/2018/November%202018/201811_003_004_038_Reducing%20Leakage%20to%20Save%20Bulk%20Water%20Costs.pdf)
- DMA partitioning in the UK is reported to have cut water losses by 85% (Farley, as cited in a review; non-Balkan, long-term national programme). — [MDPI Water 16(11):1534, 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/16/11/1534)

### Inferences

**Idea A. Pressure management with PRVs and pressure zoning (PMA)**
- Mechanism: PRVs (fixed outlet, or time/flow-modulated) at the inlets of the 6-12.6 bar zones cut leak flow (FAVAD) and new bursts.
- Share of losses attacked: background leakage, and the flow of unreported leaks, across all physical losses in the high-pressure zones. Physical losses are probably about 85-90% of NRW, and the high-pressure zones carry a disproportionate share of them.
- Evidence: Gračanica, about 640 m3/day projected for EUR 15k; Kozani, 2,500 m3/day measured; Nicosia, -1,097 m3/day; Sakarya, -41% failures; bursts about -50% on average.
- Cost: about EUR 15k per installed PRV (Kosovo 2023 anchor) times 2-4 PRVs, plus chambers and pressure loggers. Local quotes needed.
- Time: about 2-4 months, including design check against the existing study, procurement and commissioning.
- Risks: customer complaints about low pressure at high points (elevations 510-673 m mean the critical point must be logged); PRV failure or by-passing (Sakarya saw a rise in losses during a PRV failure month); fire-flow requirements. Does nothing on its own for zero-bill or old meters.
- Jury fit: situation analysis, high (uses the 12.6 bar finding); DMA + pressure, very high; IoT, medium unless a controller and loggers are added; KPIs, high (MNF, pressure, bursts); pilot feasibility, very high; economics, very high (payback in months); presentation, high (a simple story).

**Idea B. MNF-driven active leakage control (step testing plus acoustic loggers, then repairs)**
- Mechanism: use the new inlet meter's night flow to size losses; step tests isolate sections; lift-and-shift loggers or a correlator pinpoint leaks; repair the backlog.
- Share attacked: detectable leaks and bursts (unreported), likely a large share given about 1,600 m3/day input against about 480 billed.
- Evidence: Nicosia active leakage control in DMA 15 saved 266,450 m3/yr; 54 leaks found, -26,230 m3/month; Jakarta, -60 L/s from big leaks alone; Eislingen, -30% in 3 months (vendor).
- Cost: logger purchase at roughly £250 (UK) to under US$200 per unit, or hire; surveys about US$100/km (Jordan) to US$595/mile (US); repair costs per leak not sourced.
- Time: 1-3 months for the first sweep.
- Risks: leaks return quickly at 12.6 bar unless pressure is also managed (the guidance sequence); PE/PVC pipes need denser logger spacing; repair crew capacity.
- Jury fit: situation, high; DMA + pressure, medium (DMA yes, pressure no); IoT, medium-high; KPIs, high; pilot, very high; economics, high; presentation, medium.

**Idea C. Smart/AMR metering with consumption-versus-inflow reconciliation (apparent-loss attack)**
- Mechanism: replace the 485 meters older than 10 years with AMR/AMI meters, focused on the 250 zero-bill accounts; reconcile hourly or daily against the inlet meter.
- Share attacked: apparent losses only, probably about 7-13% of NRW in Kçiq by my upper-bound sketch. Its main extra value is turning the DMA balance into a near-real-time signal.
- Evidence: under-registration 5-15% (non-Balkan); quickest payback for commercial losses in general (World Bank); Selangor replaced 119,000 meters for a 50,000 m3/day commercial-loss cut (non-Balkan).
- Cost: per-meter cost not sourced for the Balkans; probably the highest capex of all ideas if all 1,031 accounts are fitted (inference).
- Time: 3-6 months for procurement and rollout.
- Risks: attacks the minority of losses; if zero-bill accounts are vacant (emigrant households), little revenue is recovered; radio coverage in pits.
- Jury fit: situation, medium; DMA + pressure, low; IoT, very high; KPIs, high; pilot, medium; economics, medium-low for this DMA; presentation, high (looks "smart").

**Idea D. "Intelligent DMA" monitoring: inlet meter plus repaired reservoir telemetry plus pressure loggers plus MNF alarms and a dashboard**
- Mechanism: continuous MNF, pressure-at-critical-point and reservoir-level monitoring with automatic alerts for new bursts. This cuts the awareness time of the "awareness, location, repair" cycle that drives leak volume.
- Share attacked: run-time of new bursts and leaks. Does not reduce existing background leakage by itself.
- Evidence: IWA DMA guidance (MNF data checks; permanent leakage and pressure control as the first step); permanent acoustic monitoring with 12-18 month ROI (vendor-reported); virtual-DMA practice.
- Cost: low to medium (loggers, GSM/LoRa, software). Fixing the damaged Notus cable is a quick, cheap prerequisite.
- Time: 1-2 months.
- Risks: alarms without repair capacity save nothing; data-quality problems such as an oversized inlet meter at low night flows.
- Jury fit: situation, high; DMA + pressure, medium; IoT, very high; KPIs, very high; pilot, very high; economics, medium (the benefit comes from the actions it triggers); presentation, high.

**Idea E. Sectorisation into sub-DMAs (one per village, or per pressure band)**
- Mechanism: split the two-village DMA into 2-4 metered sub-zones with boundary valves and sub-meters, so losses can be pinned to an area and pressure set per zone.
- Share attacked: an enabler for A and B. It does not cut losses by itself, apart from small savings at formation (Kozani: DMA formation saved 144,595 m3/yr).
- Evidence: Kozani; UK partitioning -85% over a long national programme (non-Balkan); GIZ "not a quick fix".
- Cost: meters, valves and chambers per sub-zone; not sourced for the Balkans.
- Time: 3-6 months.
- Risks: closed boundary valves create dead ends (water quality), loss of supply resilience, unknown valve condition.
- Jury fit: situation, medium; DMA + pressure, very high; IoT, medium; KPIs, medium-high; pilot, medium; economics, medium; presentation, medium.

**Idea F. Service-connection replacement programme (targeted)**
- Mechanism: replace failing service pipes and fittings, the dominant location of leaks in many systems.
- Share attacked: service-connection leaks; potentially large given 1,031 connections and 26 km of DN40.
- Evidence: 75% of 11,000 Selangor repairs were on services (non-Balkan); asset replacement is the least cost-effective leakage tool (AWA 2018, non-Balkan).
- Cost: highest per m3/day saved; not sourced for the Balkans.
- Time: longer than 6 months for meaningful coverage.
- Risks: high capex, slow, disruptive; at 12.6 bar new services can fail again without pressure management.
- Jury fit: situation, medium; DMA + pressure, low; IoT, low; KPIs, medium; pilot, low; economics, low; presentation, low-medium.

**Ranked recommendation (inference)**
1. **A + D + a one-off B campaign, branded as one idea: "Pressure-Managed Intelligent DMA Kçiq".** The core is pressure zoning with PRVs. The smart layer is inlet meter plus telemetry plus MNF and pressure alarms. The launch campaign is a step test and acoustic sweep to clear the backlog. Why: it targets the dominant part of the losses (physical leakage at excessive pressure); it carries the only Kosovo-specific evidence (Gračanica, EUR 15k for one PRV, payback under 2 months, model) plus measured SEE results (Kozani 2,500 m3/day; Nicosia -1,097 m3/day; Sakarya -41% failures); it matches IWA/GIZ sequencing (quantify, clear the backlog, manage pressure so leaks do not recur); it fits within 3-6 months; and it scores on every jury criterion, including the 35% for DMA + pressure (20%) and IoT (15%) and the 20% for KPIs (MNF m3/h, NRW m3/day, L/connection/day, ILI, burst count, AZP/AZNP, minimum pressure at the critical point).
2. **B alone (MNF-led active leakage control).** Fast and evidence-rich (Nicosia), but leaks reappear at 12.6 bar, and it misses the pressure criterion.
3. **D alone (intelligent monitoring).** Scores well on IoT and KPIs, but its savings depend on follow-up actions.
4. **E (sub-DMAs).** A strong enabler, but a slower pilot and weaker standalone economics.
5. **C (smart/AMR meters).** A good "smart" story, but it attacks probably only about 10% of Kçiq's NRW. Keep it as a phase-2 module aimed at the 250 zero-bill and 485 old meters.
6. **F (service-connection replacement).** The worst cost-effectiveness and too slow for a 3-6 month pilot.

- Suggested pilot KPI targets (illustrative, to validate): MNF down by a set m3/h after PRV commissioning; NRW from about 1,118 m3/day toward roughly 700-800 m3/day in 6 months, which would be a cut of a few hundred m3/day, consistent in scale with Gračanica's 640 m3/day projection and Nicosia's 1,097 m3/day; new bursts down about 50% over 12 months (Thornton & Lambert average). These are my targets, not sourced outcomes.

### Gaps
- No split of Kçiq's MNF into legitimate night use and leakage is available yet. It would turn the estimated 85-90% physical-loss share into a measured figure.
- Pipe materials and burst history for Kçiq were not given, so N1 and burst-reduction predictions cannot be calibrated.
- Balkan unit costs for meters, sub-DMA chambers and repairs were not found (see Q3).
- No Balkan or Southeast European published case was found that combined PRVs, MNF analytics and acoustic sweeps in a small rural DMA of about 1,000 connections. The closest are the Cyprus/Greece DMAs and the Gračanica projection.
